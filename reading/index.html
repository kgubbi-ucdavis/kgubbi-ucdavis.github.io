How to read a research paper

You rarely need to read a paper front to back on the first pass. Read the abstract, figures, and conclusion, then try to answer four questions.

  1. What problem is the paper solving?

    State it in one sentence, in your own words. Ask why it matters and why earlier approaches were not good enough.

  2. What was the important new idea?

    Most papers have one central idea surrounded by engineering. Find it, and ask whether it is a new method, a new measurement, or a new way of framing the problem.

  3. What evidence supports the result?

    Look at the baselines, datasets, and ablations. Check whether the comparison is fair, whether the gains exceed run-to-run noise, and whether anyone has reproduced it.

  4. What assumptions, limitations, or open questions remain?

    Every result holds under conditions. Note what the authors assumed, where the method fails, and what you would try next. This is often where your own research starts.
